## 1. The dataset

In this exercise we will work with a dataset that compares patterns of genome-wide variation between egg-laying and live-bearing populations from the genus Littorina. The dataset consists of 108 individuals, of which 28 are egg-laying and the remaining 80 are live-bearing.

One useful feature of this VCF file is that it includes calls for both variant and invariant sites in the genome. This is important for the correct calculation of nucleotide diversity (π) and divergence $(d_{xy})$, which measure the proportion of nucleotide sites that differ between pairs of sequences. If we included only SNPs, invariant positions would be absent from the denominator and estimates of π and $d_{xy}$ would therefore be greatly inflated.

The data are also phased. Diploid individuals carry two chromosome copies, and at heterozygous sites we may know which two alleles an individual carries without knowing which alleles occur together on the same chromosome. Phasing attempts to infer this arrangement, allowing the two haplotypes carried by each individual to be reconstructed across the genome. In other words, it tells us which alleles at different SNPs are likely to have been inherited together on the same chromosome copy. Phasing of this dataset was performed using Beagle 2.

## 3. preparing a population map

The measures that we want to compute either estimate variation within groups (π) or between groups ($d_{xy}$ and $F_{ST}$), so we need to provide more information than just the ```.geno``` file. We also need to provide a population map (referred to as the ```popsFile``` in ```genomics_general``` framework), which is a list of all of the samples in the vcf file, along with the 'population' that they belong to.

Here I have deliberately put 'population' in inverted commas, because what we really mean by population is just the groupings that we want to make, and these can be anything that makes biological sense.

The format of the popsFile is:
```text
sample_1 popA
sample_2 popA
sample_3 popA
sample_4 popB
sample_5 popB
sample_6 popB
```
This is just a text file including the sample names and population IDs separated by whitespace. The above popsFile includes 6 samples associated with two populations (popA & popB)

In our case, we want to assign invdiuals to either the "Egglayer" or "Brooder" group.

## Supplement 1: Information about the calculation of π, $d_{xy}$ and $F_{ST}$

For two sequences, (i) and (j), their genetic distance can be thought of as:

$$
d_{ij} = \frac{\text{number of sites at which } i \neq j}
{\text{number of sites compared}}
$$

Nucleotide diversity (π) is the mean pairwise distance between chromosome copies sampled from the same population:

$$
\pi = \frac{1}{N_{\text{pairs}}}\sum_{i<j}d_{ij}
$$

Thus, π measures the average proportion of nucleotide sites at which two chromosome copies from the same population differ.

Absolute divergence $(d_{xy})$ is the mean pairwise distance between chromosome copies sampled from two different populations:

$$
d_{xy} =
\frac{1}{n_xn_y}
\sum_{i \in x}\sum_{j \in y} d_{ij}
$$

where ($n_{x}$) and ($n_{y}$) are the numbers of chromosome copies sampled from populations ($x$) and ($y$). Unlike π, which measures variation within a population, $d_{xy}$ measures sequence divergence between populations.

The $F_{ST}$ statistic calculated in this exercise compares diversity within populations with diversity in the two populations combined:

$$
F_{ST} = 1-\frac{\pi_S}{\pi_T}
$$

where $pi_{S}$ is the average diversity within the two populations, weighted by their sample sizes:

$$
\pi_S =
\frac{n_x}{n_x+n_y}\pi_x+
\frac{n_y}{n_x+n_y}\pi_y
$$

and $pi_{T}$ is the mean pairwise diversity when chromosome copies from the two populations are pooled. $F_{ST}$ therefore measures how much genetic variation is partitioned between, rather than within, populations.
